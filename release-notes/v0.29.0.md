**Ciao, ciao gaps:** ALBIS fills the detector's gaps and defective pixels from their neighbours, so an image reads as one picture.

### Added

- **Inpainting.** A new **Inpainting** section in the **Analysis** tab fills the
  gaps between the detector's modules and its defective pixels from the pixels
  around them. ALBIS fills exactly what the detector's mask marks (for a
  PILATUS CBF, its -1 and -2), so there is nothing to tune. Edges and gradients
  in the sample carry straight through a gap, and a small spot next to a gap
  does not streak across it.
- **Saturated pixels too, if you want.** **Also fill saturated pixels** is off by
  default: in imaging a saturated pixel is usually real signal, not a defect.
- **What follows it.** The image, the ROI statistics and line profile, and the
  PNG and GIF exports show the filled values. The ROI's gap and defective
  counts stay, marked "filled", and the footer shows **Inpainted** while it is
  on.
- **Inpainted TIFF and CBF.** **File → Convert Dataset** has an **Inpaint** box,
  ticked while inpainting is on: the files then hold the filled values instead
  of -1 and -2, and their header says which pixels were filled. Live images
  are filled too.

---

Update if you work with images where the module gaps get in the way: imaging,
figures, or tools that cannot cope with holes. Nothing changes until you
switch inpainting on, and the data itself is never changed. See "Fill gaps and
defective pixels" in the User Guide.
