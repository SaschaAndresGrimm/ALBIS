Gap pixels and bad pixels stop being counted as measurements — and start being
counted at all in the places they were not.

### Changed

- **Statistics no longer average the module gaps.** A PILATUS frame marks an
  inter-module gap `-1` and a broken pixel `-2` in the pixel data itself, and
  those values were going into Min, Sum, Mean, Median and Std as if they were
  counts. On the frame in our test data — 16,577 of 301,453 pixels, 5.5% of it
  — **Min** moves from -2 to 0, **Mean** from 6.204 to 6.623, **Median** from 4
  to 5 and **Std** from 18.935 to 19.396, each of them now agreeing with numpy
  over the same frame with the flagged pixels removed.

  Not a new rule so much as an old one reaching the formats it had missed: a
  frame that ships a mask array has had its flagged pixels out of the
  statistics all along. A PILATUS frame carries the same information in its
  values, and was the only kind still averaging its gaps.

  **Total pixels** still counts the whole frame, and the gap, defective and
  saturated counts still account for the difference. A line profile crossing a
  module gap now breaks rather than plunging to -1. Only `-1` and `-2`, and
  only in signed integer frames: a frame that genuinely uses negatives keeps
  its real minimum, and a float frame is left alone entirely.

### Fixed

- **Gap and defective counts are right on the first frame you open.** Every
  other figure on the panel was correct; those two read 0 until you scrolled to
  the next frame, which is a strange thing to have to discover. The mask is
  fetched before the first frame exists, so it was still counted as unavailable
  at the moment the whole-image statistics were computed from it.
- **Clear ROI** shows the whole image again instead of a panel full of dashes.
  Clearing leaves an ROI with no area, which is the state that is supposed to
  report the whole frame, but nothing recomputed it.
