**Masquerade:** a first, beta look at working with pixel masks. See which pixels the mask hides and why, fix them by hand, find bad pixels, and send the result to a detector. Please try it and tell us what works and what doesn't.

### Added

- **Beta: pixel masks.** Switch them on under **Settings → Tools → Beta:
  pixel masks**. A new **Data → Pixel Mask** section then shows the mask of
  the open data: where it came from, and how many gaps and defective pixels
  it masks.
  - **Edit on image** masks and unmasks pixels by hand with a brush, a box, a
    polygon or a circle. Alt switches between masking and unmasking, Space
    pans, and every stroke can be undone. **Discard edits** goes back to the
    data's mask.
  - **Find bad pixels…** finds hot, dead and cold pixels in the image shown,
    circles them, and adds them to the mask in one step. It works best on a
    flat-field or dark image.
  - **Save as** downloads the mask as HDF5 or TIFF, with every pixel's reason
    kept. To use a saved mask again, open its HDF5 file like any data.
  - **Send to detector…** replaces a detector's mask with this one, with
    detector control on. It first says how many pixels change, sends only
    while the detector is idle, keeps the previous mask as a backup, and reads
    the mask back to confirm.
- **A Pixel Mask section on the Detector tab**, for the detector itself.
  **Open in ALBIS** saves the detector's mask as a file in the data folder's
  `masks/` and opens it. **Clear…** unmasks all but the module gaps, and
  **Restore** brings back the mask from before the last change.

### Fixed

- **The live view shows a detector's new mask.** It kept the first mask it
  fetched, even after the mask changed on the detector.
- **A summed series keeps its gaps and saturation.** With Apply mask on, the
  sum of 32-bit frames was read as floating point, so gaps and saturated
  pixels were not recognised.
- **ALBULA HDR offers black among its mask colours**, instead of white twice.
- **The start screen no longer flashes the hint for a remote ALBIS.**
